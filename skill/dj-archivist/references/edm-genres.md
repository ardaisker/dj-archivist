# EDM genre field guide

Tempo, rhythm, structure (arrangement, phrases, DJ version), role, mixing and neighbours for
the main dance music genres. BPM ranges are typical, not limits. A bar lasts 240 / BPM
seconds, so 32 bars is about a minute at 124 to 128 BPM; a blend is the overlap in which both
tracks are audible. References are long-established starting points, not a canon.

## Genre is a label, not a law

- Genre words describe scenes and eras as much as sounds, and they drift. "Garage" means New
  York and New Jersey garage house to many American DJs and UK garage to British ones;
  "progressive house" has meant hypnotic 1990s UK club music, 2010s festival EDM and, in
  stores today, melodic rolling club tracks; "electro" is not "electro house".
- Stores file each release under one category, picked by the label or distributor, sometimes
  for chart visibility. Beatport combines styles ("Melodic House & Techno", "Minimal / Deep
  Tech", "UK Garage / Bassline"), splits techno into "Peak Time / Driving" and "Raw / Deep /
  Hypnotic", and revises its list from time to time (Downtempo was split out of Organic House,
  for example). Bandcamp uses tags chosen by artists and labels.
- One track can honestly belong to two genres, and scenes coin names faster than stores adopt
  them.
- For an archive: keep a small, stable genre vocabulary for crates, store the shop genre
  separately if useful, and describe tracks by how they work in a set (tempo, rhythm, energy,
  vocals). Say so when a boundary is a matter of taste.

## House

Chicago in the 1980s, built from disco, soul and drum machines (Frankie Knuckles, Marshall
Jefferson, Larry Heard; labels Trax Records, DJ International), with a soulful New York and New
Jersey strand, garage house (Masters At Work; label Strictly Rhythm). Shared traits:
four-on-the-floor kick, off-beat hats, claps or snares on 2 and 4, 8-bar phrases grouped into
16 and 32. Club versions carry 16 to 32 bars of beat-led intro and outro and run roughly 5 to
7.5 minutes. Default mix: long EQ blend, bass swap on a phrase start, one vocal at a time.

### Deep house

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 115-124 BPM; soft kick, swung hats, jazz and soul chords (sevenths,
  ninths), warm bass, often vocals.
- **Structure:** gradual 16- and 32-bar sections, modest breakdowns; DJ versions open on
  drums, or drums and bass; dubs are common.
- **Role:** warm-up, early hours, closing, terraces.
- **Mixing:** 32- to 64-bar blends when keys agree (the chords make key matter). Pads and warm
  bass pile up in the low mids: hold the incoming low and low-mid down until the swap. Match
  swing or keep overlaps short.
- **References:** Larry Heard (Mr. Fingers), Kerri Chandler, Moodymann; labels Trax Records,
  KDJ.
- **Neighbours:** organic, afro, nu-disco, soulful house, deep tech.

### Tech house

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 124-128 BPM, some faster; punchy kick, short rolling basslines, dense
  percussion, vocal chops, little harmony.
- **Structure:** 32-bar sections, one or two breakdowns with build and drop; 32 bars of drums
  at each end.
- **Role:** warm-up to peak in house-led rooms.
- **Mixing:** the bassline is the groove, so the bass swap is the transition; percussion
  layers well for 32 to 64 bars. Basslines are pitched, so check keys; never stack two vocal
  hooks.
- **References:** Jamie Jones, Patrick Topping, Fisher, Chris Lake; labels Hot Creations,
  Toolroom, Dirtybird.
- **Neighbours:** house, deep tech, bass house, driving techno, UK funky.

### Progressive house

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 120-128 BPM; rolling 16th-note basslines, slowly layered pads and
  arpeggios. Three lives: hypnotic 1990s UK club music, 2010s festival progressive (126-128
  BPM, big breakdown and drop), today's store category (melodic, rolling, deep).
- **Structure:** long builds, 32-bar sections, extended breakdowns; underground originals
  often run 7 to 10 minutes with intros of 32 bars or more, festival extended mixes are
  shorter.
- **Role:** warm-up and journey sets; the festival style is peak-time.
- **Mixing:** patient blends of 64 to 128 bars; pads and arpeggios make key important. Swap
  the bass on a 32-bar boundary; never overlap two breakdowns.
- **References:** Sasha, John Digweed, Hernán Cattaneo, Eric Prydz; labels Bedrock,
  Renaissance. Festival era: Swedish House Mafia, Avicii; labels Axtone, Size.
- **Neighbours:** melodic house and techno, deep house, progressive trance.

### Afro house

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 118-125 BPM; four-on-the-floor under polyrhythmic percussion (congas,
  shakers, toms), chanted or sung vocals, deep pads. Grew out of South African house.
- **Structure:** percussion-led, long hypnotic sections, builds rather than big drops; 6 to 8
  minutes with 16- to 32-bar percussion intros.
- **Role:** warm-up to peak; strong at sunset and outdoors.
- **Mixing:** two dense percussion layers turn to clutter: let one track lead and thin the
  other's highs and mids. Chants and pads make key matter.
- **References:** Black Coffee, Culoe De Song, Keinemusik; labels Soulistic Music,
  Keinemusik, MoBlack Records.
- **Neighbours:** deep, organic, melodic and tech house; amapiano (typically 110-115 BPM,
  log-drum bass) with a tempo bridge.

### Melodic house and techno

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 118-126 BPM, the techno end higher; straight 16th-note arpeggios,
  rolling basslines, emotional chord progressions.
- **Structure:** a long build, one big breakdown carrying the hook, then the drop; 32-bar
  sections; 6 to 8 minutes with 32-bar intros.
- **Role:** mid to peak at melodic events; mid-set in broader house nights.
- **Mixing:** the most key-sensitive club style: arpeggios in unrelated keys clash at once.
  Blend over drum sections, keep melodic overlaps to compatible keys, high-pass the outgoing
  track, swap the bass on the phrase.
- **References:** Tale Of Us, Stephan Bodzin, Dixon, Âme, Solomun; labels Afterlife,
  Innervisions, Diynamic, Anjunadeep.
- **Neighbours:** progressive house, driving techno, progressive trance, indie dance.

### Jackin house

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 122-128 BPM; Chicago-rooted swing, chopped disco, funk and soul
  samples, vocal snippets, hard drum machines.
- **Structure:** loop-driven, changes every 8 or 16 bars; the opening loop is the way in.
- **Role:** party energy, mid to peak.
- **Mixing:** 16- to 32-bar blends, quick cuts and loops on phrase starts; match the swing.
  Sampled chords can sit between keys: trust ears over tags.
- **References:** DJ Sneak, Derrick Carter, Green Velvet (also as Cajmere); labels Cajual and
  Relief, Classic Music Company.
- **Neighbours:** tech house, funky house, disco edits, speed garage.

### Disco and nu-disco

- **Tempo and rhythm:** originals roughly 105-130 BPM, often drifting (live drummers);
  nu-disco and edits 110-124. Four-on-the-floor, off-beat open hi-hat, octave basslines,
  strings, horns, vocals.
- **Structure:** song form, sometimes with irregular phrase lengths; the DJ versions are
  12-inch mixes and edits with longer intros, drum breaks and loops.
- **Role:** warm-up, day parties, early peak, closing; familiar vocals lift a room.
- **Mixing:** originals need a variable-tempo beatgrid or hands-on beatmatching; blend 8 to 32
  bars, ideally over drum breaks. Dense harmony and vocals are the main risks.
- **References:** Chic, Donna Summer with Giorgio Moroder, Sylvester; labels Salsoul, West
  End, Prelude. Nu-disco: Todd Terje, Lindstrøm, Purple Disco Machine; label Glitterbox.
- **Neighbours:** funky and jackin house, deep house, indie dance, balearic.

### Funky and groovy house

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 122-128 BPM; filtered disco and funk loops, bouncy basslines, pianos,
  vocal hooks. Covers French filter house and 2000s vocal funky house (not UK funky).
- **Structure:** loop-based, with filter sweeps doing the building; 16- to 32-bar loop
  intros.
- **Role:** peak-time party, day events, closing.
- **Mixing:** filters are native: sweep the outgoing low end away as the incoming loop enters.
  Sampled loops with different chords clash, so keep those overlaps short.
- **References:** Daft Punk (early), Stardust, Bob Sinclar, Armand Van Helden; labels Roulé,
  Defected.
- **Neighbours:** jackin house, nu-disco, tech house.

## UK garage and relatives

London, mid-1990s: American garage house sped up and reshaped by jungle's bass weight and
pirate-radio culture (MCs, rewinds). Vocals are central, usually chopped and pitched R&B; dubs
are DJ staples. Blends run shorter than in house (16 to 32 bars) because vocals and swing are
busy.

### 2-step

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 130-138 BPM; the kick skips beats (syncopated kicks under snares on 2
  and 4), heavily swung hats, deep sub.
- **Structure:** song-like 8- and 16-bar sections; club mixes of 5 to 6 minutes with 16-bar
  drum intros, often plus a dub.
- **Role:** groove and vocal moments; anthems can peak.
- **Mixing:** swing and kick patterns vary and flam when stacked: blend over sparse intros,
  swap on the phrase, never two vocals. Cuts and rewinds are part of the culture.
- **References:** MJ Cole, Artful Dodger, Todd Edwards, Zed Bias, DJ EZ; label Locked On.
- **Neighbours:** 4x4 garage, UK funky, grime and dubstep at 138-140, future garage.

### Speed garage

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 128-136 BPM; four-on-the-floor with garage shuffle, heavy modulated
  basslines, time-stretched vocals, dub and jungle samples.
- **Structure:** 16-bar sections around bass drops; 5 to 7 minutes, 16- to 32-bar drum
  intros.
- **Role:** the peak of a garage set; a bridge from house.
- **Mixing:** bass-led: one bassline at a time, decisive swaps; mixes cleanly with house at
  the same tempo.
- **References:** Armand Van Helden (his "Spin Spin Sugar" remix for Sneaker Pimps is a
  template), Double 99, 187 Lockdown.
- **Neighbours:** house, bassline, 2-step, bass house.

### Bassline

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 135-142 BPM; from Sheffield and the north of England (the Niche club
  sound): a four-on-the-floor garage beat with the bassline as lead (wobbling, pitch-bent,
  hook-like), pitched-up vocals, organ stabs.
- **Structure:** short, drop-focused 8- and 16-bar sections; 16-bar drum intros.
- **Role:** high-energy, bouncy peak.
- **Mixing:** 8- to 16-bar blends and hard bass swaps on the phrase; the basslines are
  melodies, so key clashes show even in a quick swap.
- **References:** T2, H "Two" O, DJ Q.
- **Neighbours:** speed garage, 4x4 garage, grime; bass house with a tempo bridge.

### UK funky

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 126-132 BPM, most around 130; London, late 2000s: syncopated,
  percussion-led drums (soca, afrobeat and broken-beat influences), kicks that sometimes
  skip, garage and grime MC culture.
- **Structure:** loopy 16-bar sections, 16- to 32-bar percussion intros.
- **Role:** mid to peak; bridges house and garage.
- **Mixing:** 16- to 32-bar blends; stacked percussion clutters, so thin the outgoing highs
  and mids and swap the bass on the phrase.
- **References:** Crazy Cousinz (with Kyla on "Do You Mind"), Roska, Donae'o.
- **Neighbours:** afro house, tech house, 4x4 garage.

## Techno

Detroit in the 1980s: the Belleville Three (Juan Atkins, Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson), then
Underground Resistance and Carl Craig; Berlin (Tresor, later Ostgut Ton) and the UK carried it
further. Kick-led, repetitive, hypnotic tracks built as tools. Original mixes are the DJ
versions: 32- to 64-bar kick-and-percussion intros and outros, typically 6 to 8 minutes. DJs
layer for minutes, often on three or four decks. Percussive tracks carry little harmony, but
rumbles, stabs and drones are pitched.

### Detroit and classic techno

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 120-135 BPM; machine funk: TR-808 and TR-909 drums, synth strings and
  pads, electro and funk roots.
- **Structure:** 16- and 32-bar sections, slowly evolving chords.
- **Role:** mid-set; classics as emotional release.
- **Mixing:** 32- to 64-bar blends; strings and chords need key care.
- **References:** Juan Atkins, Kevin Saunderson, Jeff Mills, Carl Craig, Underground
  Resistance; labels Metroplex, Transmat, KMS, Planet E.
- **Neighbours:** electro, deep house, dub techno, driving techno.

### Minimal

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 120-128 BPM; sparse micro-variation: clicks, swung percussion,
  understated bass. Spans stripped-down Detroit techno and European microhouse.
- **Structure:** slow change over 32- and 64-bar spans; long originals with long intros.
- **Role:** warm-up, after-hours, hypnotic stretches.
- **Mixing:** blends of 64 bars or more with small EQ moves; match groove and swing. Quiet
  subs still add up.
- **References:** Robert Hood, Richie Hawtin (Plastikman), Ricardo Villalobos; labels M_nus,
  Perlon, Kompakt.
- **Neighbours:** deep tech, tech house, dub techno.

### Melodic techno

Covered under melodic house and techno above.

### Peak-time, driving and hard techno

- **Tempo and rhythm:** driving and peak-time 128-138 BPM; hard techno 140-160 and beyond.
  Big compressed or distorted kicks, rumbling sub, relentless percussion; hard techno borrows
  from schranz (German hard techno), gabber, trance and rave.
- **Structure:** tension and release through filtering and layering; 16- to 32-bar kick
  intros.
- **Role:** peak time, late night.
- **Mixing:** driving techno takes 32- to 64-bar layers; at hard-techno tempos blends shorten
  to 16 to 32 bars and kick swaps must be decisive, because two distorted kicks smear. These
  masters are extremely loud: watch the headroom.
- **References:** Adam Beyer, Ben Klock, Marcel Dettmann, Charlotte de Witte, Amelie Lens;
  labels Drumcode, Ostgut Ton, KNTXT, Tresor.
- **Neighbours:** tech house (light end), industrial, hard trance; hardstyle and hardcore
  (fast end).

### Industrial techno

- **Tempo and rhythm:** roughly 125-140 BPM, sometimes faster; distortion, noise, metallic
  percussion, EBM influence, some broken rhythms.
- **Structure:** textural loops, changes every 16 or 32 bars, long kick intros.
- **Role:** late night, dark rooms.
- **Mixing:** noise layers well; two distorted low ends do not. Medium blends, careful gain.
- **References:** Surgeon, Regis, Perc, Paula Temple; labels Downwards, Perc Trax.
- **Neighbours:** EBM, hard techno, electro.

## Trance

Took shape in Germany and wider Europe in the early 1990s, then became a global festival sound
through Dutch and British labels. Off-beat or rolling basslines, supersaw leads and arpeggios,
long melodic breakdowns, snare-roll builds. Extended mixes carry 32- to 64-bar intros and
outros (about 6 to 9 minutes). Melodies are the point, so key discipline matters. Default mix:
intro under outro, bass swap on the phrase, out before or after the outgoing breakdown; never a
breakdown over the other track's melody.

### Progressive trance

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 128-136 BPM; rolling bass, restrained melodies.
- **Structure:** 32-bar sections, long intros, gradual breakdowns.
- **Role:** warm-up and build.
- **Mixing:** 32- to 64-bar blends, as in progressive house.
- **References:** Markus Schulz; much of the style appears on Anjunabeats and Armada Music.
- **Neighbours:** progressive house, melodic techno, uplifting trance.

### Uplifting trance

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 136-140 BPM; off-beat bass, euphoric chord progressions.
- **Structure:** 16- and 32-bar builds into a long breakdown and climax; intros of 32 bars or
  more.
- **Role:** peak, anthems.
- **Mixing:** 32- to 64-bar blends over drums; keys must agree before the climax.
- **References:** Armin van Buuren, Paul van Dyk, Ferry Corsten, Above & Beyond; labels
  Armada Music, Anjunabeats, Black Hole Recordings.
- **Neighbours:** progressive trance, hard trance, psytrance.

### Psytrance

- **Tempo and rhythm:** most dancefloor styles 134-148 BPM (progressive psy lower, full-on
  higher, dark and forest styles faster, hi-tech far faster); rooted in Goa trance. A short,
  punchy kick with a rolling bassline in the gaps (typically three 16th notes between kicks),
  acid lines, psychedelic effects.
- **Structure:** 7 to 9 minutes, 8- and 16-bar sections, long kick-and-bass intros.
- **Role:** all-night peak at psy events; a harder push in trance sets.
- **Mixing:** kick and bass are one interlocked pattern, so two basslines at once wreck the
  groove. Swap the lows on an 8- or 16-bar boundary and blend 16 to 32 bars in the highs and
  mids; bass notes are pitched, so key matters.
- **References:** Infected Mushroom, Astrix, Hallucinogen, Vini Vici; labels TIP Records,
  Dragonfly Records, Iboga Records.
- **Neighbours:** progressive psy with techno and progressive trance; full-on with uplifting
  trance.

## Drum and bass and jungle

### Drum and bass

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 170-176 BPM, most modern tracks 172-175; a two-step breakbeat with
  snares on 2 and 4, often felt as a half-time groove around 86-88; deep sub. Styles include
  liquid, neurofunk, jump-up, rollers and dancefloor.
- **Structure:** 16- and 32-bar blocks (intro, drop, breakdown, second drop, outro); 16- to
  32-bar intros, sometimes 64; roughly 4.5 to 6.5 minutes.
- **Role:** a whole night in itself; in mixed sets a tempo jump (cut, or bridge through
  half-time at about 87).
- **Mixing:** fast: run the incoming intro under the last 16 to 32 bars and drop it on a
  phrase; transitions often come every one to three minutes. Double drops need bass
  management: kill one track's lows or pick basslines that agree.
- **References:** Goldie, LTJ Bukem, Roni Size, Andy C, Noisia; labels Metalheadz, Good
  Looking, V Recordings, RAM Records, Hospital Records.
- **Neighbours:** jungle, halftime, dubstep (by feel), footwork.

### Jungle

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 160-175 BPM; chopped, re-sequenced breakbeats (the Amen break above
  all), reggae and dancehall samples, heavy sub. Grew out of UK breakbeat hardcore in the
  early 1990s.
- **Structure:** 16-bar blocks, break edits; 16- to 32-bar intros, often breaks alone.
- **Role:** peak energy; the rootsier strand of a drum and bass set.
- **Mixing:** breaks on breaks get busy: blend 8 to 32 bars and thin one track's highs and
  mids. Rewinds are part of the culture.
- **References:** Congo Natty, Shy FX, DJ Hype; labels Reinforced, Moving Shadow.
- **Neighbours:** drum and bass, breakbeat hardcore, footwork.

## Dubstep and its descendants

- **Tempo and rhythm:** 138-142 BPM, usually 140, in half-time (kick on 1, snare on 3), so it
  feels like 70; sparse, sub-heavy, dub-influenced. From south London (Croydon) in the early
  2000s, out of 2-step, dub, grime and drum and bass.
- **Structure:** 16- and 32-bar blocks, drops on the phrase, a varied second drop; 16- to
  32-bar intros; VIP versions are common.
- **Role:** peak energy at bass nights; a half-time change of feel in mixed sets.
- **Mixing:** 8- to 32-bar blends, frequent double drops. Sub bass sums dangerously: cut one
  track's lows and watch the master. Sub notes are pitched, so key matters.
- **Descendants:** brostep (aggressive mid-range bass, build-and-drop structure: Skrillex,
  Excision, Rusko; labels OWSLA, Never Say Die), riddim (minimal, repetitive bass patterns),
  tearout, melodic dubstep, deep dubstep or "140" (the original sound continued), future
  garage and UK bass hybrids.
- **References:** Skream, Benga, Digital Mystikz (Mala and Coki), Kode9, Burial, Pinch;
  labels DMZ, Tempa, Hyperdub, Deep Medi Musik, Tectonic.
- **Neighbours:** grime and 2-step at 138-140, trap (half-time, 140-150), halftime drum and
  bass.

## Breaks and breakbeat

- **Tempo and rhythm:** club breaks 125-140 BPM, big beat often slower (around 100-130);
  broken, syncopated drums from sampled or programmed funk breaks, electro and acid
  basslines. Strands: big beat (late-1990s UK), nu skool breaks (early 2000s), Florida breaks,
  rave-breaks revivals, UK bass hybrids.
- **Structure:** 8- and 16-bar sections; 16- to 32-bar drum intros, sometimes
  four-on-the-floor.
- **Role:** flexible, mid to peak; a bridge between house or techno and bass music.
- **Mixing:** two broken beats flam when overlapped: keep blends to 8 to 16 bars or use
  four-on-the-floor intros and outros. Sampled breaks do not always sit tight on the grid:
  check it.
- **References:** The Chemical Brothers, Fatboy Slim, The Prodigy, Stanton Warriors, Adam
  Freeland; labels Skint, Wall of Sound, Marine Parade.
- **Neighbours:** electro, techno, UK bass, house.

## Electro

- **Tempo and rhythm:** typically 120-135 BPM, early electro-funk often slower; TR-808 drums,
  syncopated kicks with snares or claps on 2 and 4, robotic basslines, vocoders. Lineage:
  Kraftwerk, early-1980s US electro-funk, Detroit's darker electro, a long European line. Not
  electro house, the distorted-synth club sound of the 2000s (Justice, Benny Benassi).
- **Structure:** 8- and 16-bar sections; many tracks are short with brief intros, so loops
  help.
- **Role:** warm-up curveballs, techno sets, dark late-night moments.
- **Mixing:** syncopated kicks fill a four-on-the-floor grid, so electro layers well against
  techno; cut the lows on one track, since long 808 subs are pitched. 16- to 32-bar blends.
- **References:** Cybotron, Egyptian Lover, Drexciya, Aux 88, DJ Stingray; labels Underground
  Resistance, Clone.
- **Neighbours:** techno, breaks, Miami bass, EBM.

## Hardstyle and hardcore (in brief)

- **Hardstyle:** 145-160 BPM, most often about 150; Dutch origins around 2000. The distorted,
  pitched kick is the bass (a "reverse bass" fills the off-beat); euphoric melodies; the
  breakdown ends in a kick drop. DJ versions have 16- to 32-bar kick intros. Mix one kick at a
  time with short blends (8 to 16 bars) or cuts; keys matter because kicks are tuned.
  References: Headhunterz, Brennan Heart, Noisecontrollers; Scantraxx, Dirty Workz, Q-dance.
- **Hardcore:** roughly 160-200 BPM (UK and happy hardcore around 165-180; uptempo and
  speedcore beyond 200); gabber roots in early-1990s Rotterdam. Short kick-led blends and hard
  cuts. References: Paul Elstak, Angerfist, Neophyte; Rotterdam Records, Masters of Hardcore.
- **Neighbours:** hard techno and hard trance at 145-160; hardstyle into hardcore through
  gradual tempo ramps.

## Downtempo and organic house (in brief)

- **Downtempo:** roughly 70-110 BPM; trip-hop, chillout and electronica with varied, often
  broken rhythms, for openings, lounges and after-hours. Mix with crossfades, echo-outs and
  phrase-aware overlaps; beatmatching is optional when drums are sparse. References: Massive
  Attack, Portishead, Bonobo, Nightmares On Wax; labels Ninja Tune, Warp.
- **Organic house:** roughly 110-124 BPM; soft kicks, hand percussion, acoustic and
  non-Western instruments, melodic pads; 16- to 32-bar percussion intros. Warm-up, sunset,
  daytime. Long, key-aware blends. References: Sabo, Lee Burridge; labels Sol Selectas, All
  Day I Dream.
- **Neighbours:** deep, afro and melodic house, and each other.

## Moving between genres

- Climb the quick-reference table in small tempo steps; half and double tempo link distant
  genres (70 with 140, 87 with 174; see `music-theory-for-djs.md`).
- Rhythm changes are harder than tempo changes: go from four-on-the-floor to a broken beat
  through a cut, a breakdown or a sparse intro, and flag versatile bridge tracks in the
  archive.

## Version names

Labels are hints; the length and the intro tell you what you actually have
(bars = length in seconds × BPM / 240).

| Name | Usually | Length and structure | DJ use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original Mix | The producer's main version; in club genres, the club arrangement | Typically 5 to 8 min in house and techno, beat-led intro and outro | Default in underground dance music; some dance-pop releases use the name for a radio-length version, so check the length |
| Extended Mix | Club version of a track released mainly as a radio edit | Adds 16- to 32-bar beat intros and outros, often a longer breakdown | Default for EDM, trance and dance-pop; files with this label can be different edits |
| Radio Edit | Short version for radio and streaming | About 2.5 to 4 min; starts near the hook, fades or ends abruptly | Hard to mix; fine for cuts or with a loop |
| Club Mix | Version aimed at clubs; on older releases the 12-inch mix, sometimes a rework | Usually extended length | Usually DJ-friendly |
| Dub | Stripped version, vocals removed or reduced to fragments | Similar to the vocal mix, more space | Long blends, layering acapellas; a house and UK garage favourite |
| VIP | Rework by the original producer (usually glossed as "variation in production"), from drum and bass and dubstep dubplate culture | Genre-standard intro, new or harder drop | A separate track: tag it clearly |
| Edit, re-edit, rework | Rearrangement of an existing recording | Extended intros, looped grooves, cut sections | Disco and funk edits are the classic case |
| Bootleg | Unofficial, usually uncleared remix or edit | Varies | Free, unsold, can disappear; check file quality |
| DJ-pool Intro | Radio or album version with a beat-only intro (commonly 8 to 16 bars), sometimes an outro | Around 3 to 5 min | For open-format DJs; mixable but shorter than a club version |
| Clean and Dirty | Clean mutes or replaces explicit words; Dirty is uncensored | Identical lengths | Choose per venue; length cannot tell them apart |

Also: quick hits and short edits (fast mixing), acapellas and instrumentals (layering),
transition edits (a tempo change inside the track), remasters (loudness and sometimes length
differ) and "(Mixed)" versions from DJ-mix compilations, which start and end inside someone
else's transitions and do not belong in an archive.
