# Transitions and effects: coaching reference

Reference hardware: a 2-channel controller such as the Pioneer DDJ-FLX4 in Rekordbox
Performance mode (3-band EQ Hi/Mid/Low, filter knob per channel, Smart Fader, Smart CFX,
Beat FX, 8 performance pads per deck: Hot Cue, Pad FX, Beat Jump, Sampler). Button names
below are for that controller; map them to the DJ's own hardware before teaching.

## Transition families (teaching ladder, in order)

Teach one rung at a time. Each rung assumes the previous is comfortable.

### 1. EQ swap (the foundation)
Bring track B in with Low fully cut, Mid slightly cut. Over 16 to 32 bars, swap the lows:
cut A's Low as you open B's Low **on a downbeat, in one confident move**. Two basslines
coexisting is mud; the swap moment is binary even when everything else is gradual.
Drill: pick two same-key tracks, blend for 32 bars, swap lows at bar 16.
You've got it when: a listener can't point to the moment the bassline changed hands.

### 2. Bassline swap on phrase boundaries
Same as EQ swap, but timed to the genre's 16/32-bar phrase structure (e.g. UK garage:
intro/verse/drop sections). Learn to count phrases while beatmatched. Drill: call out "phrase" out loud
every 16 bars for a full track, then blend with swaps only on phrase starts.

### 3. Filter fade
Use the channel filter (HPF direction) on the outgoing track to thin it while the
incoming track fills the space from below. More gradual and hypnotic than a hard EQ
swap: the plateau-set workhorse. Danger: resonance buildup near the cutoff; keep the
sweep slow and finish with the Low kill anyway.

### 4. Loop roll exit
Set a 4-beat (then 2, then 1) loop on the outgoing track's last phrase, tighten the
loop to build tension, cut to the incoming drop. This is an energy tool: use it at the
apex or into the peak run, not in the hypnotic core.

FLX4 loop controls (get these exact; teaching wrong buttons breaks trust):
**IN/4BEAT** (hold ~1s) starts a 4-beat auto loop; **CUE/LOOP CALL ◄ (LOOP 1/2X)**
halves the active loop (this is the roll motor, NOT Shift+IN); **CUE/LOOP CALL ►
(LOOP 2X)** doubles it; **RELOOP/EXIT** exits. Quantize on so halvings sit on the beat.
A Roll-type Pad FX preserves phrase position on exit; a manual loop resumes from the
loop point.

### 5. Echo out
Beat FX Echo (1/2 or 3/4 beat), engage on the outgoing track's final downbeat, kill
the channel fader; the echo tail decays over the incoming track's intro. The cleanest
way out of a harmonic dead-end: keep the echo short (1/2 beat) or filter it, because the
tail repeats the notes it caught and only clears the key once it has faded. One per set reads intentional;
three reads like an escape hatch.

### 6. Drop swap
Both tracks cued so their drops align; cut (crossfader or fader) directly from A's
last build into B's drop. High risk, high reward; club-set tool. Requires perfect
beatgrids and rehearsal; this is a rehearsed move, not an improvised one.

## Smart Fader / Smart CFX (FLX4-specific)

Smart Fader auto-syncs BPM and applies EQ/echo through the crossfader: fine for
parties, but it flattens exactly the skills the ladder builds. Rule: practice with it
OFF; perform with it off too once rung 2 is solid. Smart CFX is a per-knob macro
effect; treat it like any effect below.

## Effect taste rules

- Effects serve the transition or the phrase: if an effect is audible as "an effect"
  for more than a bar or two outside a transition, it's ego, cut it.
- In focus/plateau sets, effects budget is lower: filter and subtle echo only; loop
  rolls and heavy Beat FX break the flow trance.
- Pad FX: pick 2 pads (e.g. echo + low-cut filter) and learn them to muscle memory
  rather than browsing all 8 mid-set.

## Practice drill template

For any technique: (1) two named tracks from the user's crates, same or adjacent key;
(2) a specific bar count and target moment; (3) record the practice mix in Rekordbox;
(4) self-review question. Recording every practice session is non-negotiable; the
recording is also the raw material for the Set Renderer later.

## Coaching progression tracking

Keep a simple ladder status when coaching across sessions: which rung is comfortable,
which is in progress, what the last drill revealed. Advance one rung at a time; revisit
rung 1 whenever a recording shows muddy bass overlaps.
